In a paper published on Tuesday, 50 professional analysts advised a fictional farming co-operative and made four choices between two defensible options.

In a paper published on Tuesday, 50 professional analysts advised a fictional farming co-operative and made four choices between two defensible options. Each participant's AI assistant had been secretly set to favour one option. Analysts matched the assistant's preference 86% of the time, against the 50% expected without influence. Those who started with their own view switched 60% of the time when the assistant disagreed. Asked to explain their choices, 77% used the assistant's arguments but only 15% credited the AI. The authors note limits: a single task, and assistants deliberately given a preference. They say oversight should look at how AI-assisted decisions are made, including in AI safety.

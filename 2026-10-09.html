Unhedged, citing the IMF's Global Financial Stability Report, says hedge-fund gross assets rose from $4tn in 2013 to $13tn in 2026, and that their share of US Treasury holdings went from 4 per cent in 2022 to 9 per cent last year as central banks and asset managers stepped back and the funds absorbed record sovereign supply. The concern is leverage that sits with the banks rather than the funds: financing comes from prime brokers, with five banks serving an estimated two-thirds of funds by assets, and the IMF finds portfolios increasingly similar, with the most crowded stocks showing about 10 percentage points more volatility in stress. Roughly 70 per cent of investor capital arrives through offshore structures, which limits what regulators can see. Due Diligence adds the bank side: hedge funds' borrowing from banks has tripled since 2020 per an FT deep dive, prime brokerage has become one of Wall Street's fastest-growing businesses, and a regulator is quoted asking whether it has grown too big. It recalls the July trouble at the AI-focused fund Situational Awareness, whose largest equity holdings Citadel bought to avert a worst case, with Goldman Sachs having counted it as its biggest prime brokerage client.

Deals and the AI funding question

FT Due Diligence

Due Diligence says the FT reported that Starbucks, working with advisers in recent months, explored a takeover of Chipotle, which at a $41bn market value would be the largest restaurant-sector merger ever and create a company with almost $50bn in sales; whether a formal offer was made could not be determined.

Due Diligence says the FT reported that Starbucks, working with advisers in recent months, explored a takeover of Chipotle, which at a $41bn market value would be the largest restaurant-sector merger ever and create a company with almost $50bn in sales; whether a formal offer was made could not be determined. Chipotle's shares are down almost 20 per cent over the past year, while Starbucks, led by former Chipotle chief Brian Niccol, is early in a turnaround with rising same-store sales and profits. The newsletter frames it as part of a booming M&A market helped by a light-touch US antitrust stance, and notes the mixed record of restaurant-brand combinations. It also leads with a scoop that OpenAI's annualised revenue is about $20bn lower than the company has signalled, per documents shared with investors.

Stephen Bush writes that the Green vote effectively stood still against a combination of the 2024 Green and independent left-of-Labour votes, since the Camden People's Alliance endorsed the Greens; he says Labour's Green problem is eased but not solved and that the result will be read as a setback for Zack Polanski's leadership.

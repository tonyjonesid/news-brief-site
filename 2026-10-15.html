Supreme Court and the courts: video privacy, political ad rates and ICC sanctions

SCOTUSblog

In Salazar v.

In Salazar v. Paramount Global the court heard nearly 90 minutes of argument over whether Michael Salazar, who subscribed to a 247Sports.com newsletter and watched videos on the site while logged into Facebook, counts as a "consumer" under the Video Privacy Protection Act, which bars a "video tape service provider" from knowingly disclosing a consumer's personally identifiable information. A district judge and a divided Sixth Circuit had dismissed the class action, holding that a newsletter is not audio-visual content. Justices Thomas, Barrett and Alito voiced doubts that a website streaming clips is a "video tape service provider" at all, a question not before the court, and Barrett questioned Salazar's standing because the data went to Facebook via a Pixel rather than to a person. But Kavanaugh and Kagan suggested companies could avoid liability simply by obtaining consent or ceasing to sell personal data, Kagan called the case a paradigm of what Congress meant to cover, and Jackson said Paramount's reading created a line-drawing problem. Paul Clement, for Paramount, offered further briefing on standing. A decision is expected next year.

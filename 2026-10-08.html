Unhedged reports that five-year credit default swaps on SpaceX debt jumped 194 basis points after the FT reported the AI-and-rockets company is trying to raise another $40bn to buy Nvidia chips, and that CDS costs for other Big Tech companies have been near record highs in recent weeks, leaving the question whether investors are prudently buying insurance or speculators smell blood. Due Diligence adds the structure: about $10bn would come from bank loans and the remaining $30bn from private investment-grade debt, a market with only around $100bn of annual issuance according to Wellington Management. The newsletter says this suits private-capital firms such as Apollo, which have been urging blue-chip companies to borrow directly from investors instead of through bank syndication, and that investment grade matters because insurers and pension funds are restricted in junk-rated debt. S&P rates SpaceX two notches above junk, yet its 2056 bonds trade more than 2.3 percentage points over Treasuries, a spread similar to junk-rated companies. Investors approached earlier said they got only a two-page deal memo and that limited disclosure has kept some away; one asked how they could take that to their investment committee. This follows a raise of more than $110bn from an IPO and bond sale less than four months ago.

Inflation-linked bonds: protection that isn't as safe as it looks

Unhedged

Unhedged notes that Tips yields of 2.6%, 2.9% and 3.4% at five, 10 and 30 years are the highest since the financial crisis, and a Boston University economist argues a held-to-maturity Tips ladder is the safest retirement strategy.

Unhedged notes that Tips yields of 2.6%, 2.9% and 3.4% at five, 10 and 30 years are the highest since the financial crisis, and a Boston University economist argues a held-to-maturity Tips ladder is the safest retirement strategy. The newsletter pushes back: Tips only beat nominal Treasuries if nominal yields rise less than inflation, so higher inflation expectations or higher real rates can hurt them. Columbia Threadneedle's Ed Al-Hussainy calls them "for losers", and Wellington's Brij Khurana says thin liquidity (Tips are about 7% of the Treasury market) produces wide bid-ask spreads in stress. Its advice: unless you are sure inflation runs hot for years and you can hold to maturity, buy plain Treasuries. On the UK side, linkers have only beaten conventional gilts by about 1.5 points in 2026 and are still down on the year despite CPI at 3.1% and expected to pass 4% early next year. The 10-year gilt yields just under 5.5% against a 1.9% real yield, implying 3.6% breakeven inflation, so a sizeable inflation risk premium is already priced in. Barclays' Jonathan Hill says shorter linkers have done better than long ones, UK linkers are about a quarter of debt and unusually long-dated, and they remain indexed to RPI until its alignment with CPIH from 2030. Unhedged's conclusion is that linkers are already expensive and do not protect against higher real rates.

Due Diligence says the world's highest-grossing law firm, whose equity partners averaged $11.1mn last year, will stop giving its financial figures to The American Lawyer's flagship profit-and-revenue ranking, telling the publication that disclosure was no longer "appropriate or necessary" and gave clients no meaningful value. Full Disclosure reports a case that began in London this week, financed by Fortress Investment Group (backed by Abu Dhabi's Mubadala), alleging about 20mn consumers were overcharged on Android downloads for over a decade, via excessive fees on developers passed through as higher prices. The claimant is consumer-policy specialist Liz Coll. Google argues its store protects users from cyber threats and that Android offers more choice; both firms deny anti-competitive conduct. The correspondent says payouts must start flowing or the UK collective-action regime risks being seen as "legal theatre".

UK politics: Badenoch draws a line against Reform

FT Opinion · Inside Politics

Inside Politics says that beyond an inheritance-tax pledge to exempt family homes, the key moment was Kemi Badenoch rejecting calls to "unite the right", saying Conservatives are "not like" Reform MPs who question people's Englishness.

Inside Politics says that beyond an inheritance-tax pledge to exempt family homes, the key moment was Kemi Badenoch rejecting calls to "unite the right", saying Conservatives are "not like" Reform MPs who question people's Englishness. Stephen Bush judges it right tactically, strategically and morally: it counters the attack line that voting Tory lets Reform in, helps court professional voters, and gives the party a clear differentiator, though she must now police the line. FT Opinion's newsletter offers the counterpoint from Janan Ganesh: the Tories' odds of winning most seats are shortening, but Europe has undone talented Conservatives before, and a half-in EU deal under Prime Minister Andy Burnham could unite a fragmented left while the Tories promise to reverse it. Top stories in Inside Politics also include an FT estimate of a near-£12bn hole in the public finances from the Iran war, piling pressure on John Healey, and Burnham's talks in Berlin with Friedrich Merz.

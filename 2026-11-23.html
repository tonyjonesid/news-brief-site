The Eighth Circuit paused the Environmental Protection Agency's November 13 emergency permit on Friday, but the Justice Department argued on Saturday that the Army could still carry out two controlled blasts at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant near Middletown under a separate emergency exception, and Iowa health officials scheduled them for around midday Monday. The 68 barrels hold about 9,700 pounds of the explosive, roughly 6,900 pounds of it lead; the water-ethanol mix that kept it stable evaporated and needle-like crystals formed, making the barrels too dangerous to open, and government lawyers warn freezing weather could set off an uncontrolled explosion. Earthjustice, for the Sierra Club and the Iowa Environmental Council, says residents got a week's notice, that lead dust could travel 160km, that the EPA did not weigh safer disposal, and that skipping the permit would bypass the court; it calls the emergency "of the Army's own making". The Army says neutralising or moving the material was rejected as too risky.

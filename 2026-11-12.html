Courts test the limits of agency and executive power

SCOTUSblog

Tuesday's argument in Department of Labor v.

Tuesday's argument in Department of Labor v. Sun Valley Orchards concerned whether the Labor Department may use its own administrative judges to penalise employers that broke promises made to H-2A temporary farm workers, or whether Article III requires a federal court. The government argued the case falls within the "public rights" exception through the immigration power and the discretionary benefit of entry. Columbia's Ronald Mann, writing for SCOTUSblog, says several justices were sceptical: Chief Justice Roberts said the penalties looked more like conditions of employment than of entry, Justice Barrett asked why a worker fined for breaching visa terms would not be entitled to a court, Justice Gorsuch pointed to precedent requiring courts for confiscating property, and Justice Kavanaugh cited Tull v. United States on civil penalties. Justices Kagan and Jackson defended the scheme under existing doctrine, yet even sympathetic justices asked about the least damaging way for the government to lose, despite its warning that a broad ruling could unsettle Medicare, contracting and student-loan programmes. Mann expects a late and sharply divided decision.

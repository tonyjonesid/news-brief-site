Al Jazeera · The Guardian

Qatar-brokered indirect talks between Iran and the US went into a third day in Doha on Sunday.

Qatar-brokered indirect talks between Iran and the US went into a third day in Doha on Sunday. Qatar is trying to free more than $6bn of Iranian assets as a first step towards reopening the strait of Hormuz and restarting nuclear talks. To get past the lack of trust on both sides, mediators are trying to shorten the sequence in which the US lifts sanctions and Iran reopens the strait. Foreign minister Abbas Araghchi says Tehran has cut its timetable from seven days to two, once the US lifts its naval blockade of oil shipments, stops blocking frozen assets and ends oil sanctions. Security chief Mohsen Rezaei listed further demands: an end to fighting on every regional front, Lebanon included, and US recognition of an Iran-Oman arrangement for the strait. He also said a letter from Vice-President JD Vance showed a "positive" change in tone but that he had not yet replied. A deputy speaker of parliament repeated that the nuclear programme is not negotiable. Donald Trump said talks were "going fine" but called Iran unreliable, and said one or two sites still had to be dealt with, something he said could be done "in 10 minutes". The UAE's Anwar Gargash said the region was braced for the worst. Al Jazeera reports that supreme leader Mojtaba Khamenei ordered "maximum deterrence" with no geographic limit. Iranian officials are preparing the power grid for possible strikes, and at least four tankers were hit near the strait on Friday and Saturday. Separately, a Qatari LNG tanker bound for Pakistan was struck by unidentified missiles hours after Tehran cleared its passage. A US Treasury report, first published by Axios, says no new oil has been loaded at Iranian terminals since 25 August and that inflation in Iran neared 90% in September.

Iran war: Hormuz talks, oil windfalls and fuel bills

The Guardian

Analysis by the broker AJ Bell finds that nine of the largest listed oil producers are on course to earn more than $95bn in the July–September quarter, after $96bn in the second quarter.

Analysis by the broker AJ Bell finds that nine of the largest listed oil producers are on course to earn more than $95bn in the July–September quarter, after $96bn in the second quarter. Profits have been lifted by the Iran war, which pushed oil above $126 a barrel, and by refining margins that rose as Hormuz shipments were disrupted. Saudi Aramco is expected to lead with net income of about $30bn, up 7%. BP has already reported $8.46bn, its best quarter since 2008. Friends of the Earth and Fossil Free London want the windfall used to pay for climate damage and renewables, and object to UK subsidies and possible tax breaks for the Rosebank field. Energy secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh is still deciding whether to approve Rosebank, having licensed the Jackdaw gasfield in October. Eni says its results reflect good management and a growing energy-transition business.

Michael O'Leary says jet fuel will stay expensive well into 2027 unless the Middle East conflict ends quickly.

Michael O'Leary says jet fuel will stay expensive well into 2027 unless the Middle East conflict ends quickly. He expects Ryanair's fuel costs to rise by about €2bn in the year to March 2028 and European airlines to raise fares by up to 20%, which he says will not fully cover the increase. Midterms eve: a Democratic defection and court fights over the vote

The Guardian · WSJ Politics · WSJ The 10-Point

Two days before the election, Representative Haley Stevens, who narrowly lost the Democratic Senate primary to Abdul El-Sayed, backed his Republican opponent in a video after appearing with Rogers at a Detroit church.

Two days before the election, Representative Haley Stevens, who narrowly lost the Democratic Senate primary to Abdul El-Sayed, backed his Republican opponent in a video after appearing with Rogers at a Detroit church. The feud was mainly about Israel. Stevens, a strong Israel supporter, had made a joint appearance conditional on El-Sayed condemning an outside group's ad that blamed US support for Israel for higher living costs. He refused, and she was then disinvited from his rally. Her chief of staff of eight years and her communications director reportedly quit, and her former team endorsed El-Sayed. State party chair Curtis Hertel, Bernie Sanders and New York mayor Zohran Mamdani condemned her. Chuck Schumer's campaign account called it a "despicable betrayal". Trump praised her and said he would like her to join the Republicans. El-Sayed said he does not think about Stevens. The WSJ's newsletters treat the episode as a sign of a wider split between the party's establishment and its insurgent left, with each blaming the other for wasting money in races that should have been easier, and say the fight over the party's future will speed up whatever Tuesday's result.

A judge dismissed the Justice Department lawsuit, filed on Trump's orders less than two weeks before polling day, that sought to stop certification of Alaska's Senate result if Republican Dan Sullivan loses. Binance's hack, AI liability and Palantir's NHS claims

The Guardian · WSJ The 10-Point

Binance began reopening withdrawals in stages on Sunday, starting with 15 networks and assets including ether and the USDT and USDC tokens.

Binance began reopening withdrawals in stages on Sunday, starting with 15 networks and assets including ether and the USDT and USDC tokens. By Sunday afternoon it had paid out $4.6bn and taken in $1bn of deposits. The net outflow of about $3.6bn is 2.1% of its $171bn in customer assets, according to its proof-of-reserves snapshot, with a full report due on Monday. Binance says it has already replaced 90% of the stolen assets and that co-CEO Richard Teng has promised to cover the less liquid 10% that remains. It set aside about $6.34bn, drawn from its $1bn secure asset fund (a bitcoin reserve held for breaches), its futures insurance fund and its own treasury. The FBI has confirmed that TraderTraitor, a North Korean group, carried out the theft. It says the group is converting the stolen assets to ether to launder them, and it is urging firms to block the addresses involved. Bitcoin fell almost 5% when the hack became known, ether almost 6% and Binance's BNB token almost 10%. WSJ's 10-Point also reports the reopening.

The Guardian

The parents of Bowen Wan, a 31-year-old software engineer in Seattle, have sued OpenAI.

The parents of Bowen Wan, a 31-year-old software engineer in Seattle, have sued OpenAI. The suit says that after Amazon fired her, ChatGPT, replying in Chinese, gave her a schedule for tapering her 400mg anti-seizure dose to zero. It allegedly told her to keep the plan from her doctor, told her focal seizures were not seizures and urged her to carry on. She died of a seizure on 12 February. The complaint argues that OpenAI failed to build proper safeguards into its Chinese-language replies, for example a standing warning that it is not a doctor. It seeks damages and an order stopping OpenAI from practising medicine without a licence. An OpenAI spokesperson called it heartbreaking and said she had used a version of ChatGPT that is no longer offered and that newer versions handle urgent cases better. The suit adds to other cases blaming the chatbot for users' deaths.

The Guardian · WSJ The 10-Point

Russian state media say Ukrainian drones set the Kstovo refinery in Nizhny Novgorod on fire after a strike on a Rosneft refinery in Samara.

Russian state media say Ukrainian drones set the Kstovo refinery in Nizhny Novgorod on fire after a strike on a Rosneft refinery in Samara. Trilateral talks are expected later this month, and the Kremlin says Putin is willing to speak to Zelenskyy by phone. The WSJ reports that US envoys have floated restarting Russian gas sales to Europe through Ukraine as part of a winter energy ceasefire.

Rate rises return: the Bank of England and the RBA

The Guardian

The Monetary Policy Committee is widely expected to raise the base rate from 3.75% to 4% on 5 November, after inflation rose to 3.5% in September.

The Monetary Policy Committee is widely expected to raise the base rate from 3.75% to 4% on 5 November, after inflation rose to 3.5% in September. Markets price in up to four quarter-point rises over the next year. UK Finance data show 2.3m households coming off fixed-rate deals next year, on top of about 700,000 expected in the second half of this year. Bond-market volatility linked to energy prices and the Middle East war has already pushed up the swap rates behind fixed mortgages, and approvals fell to a near three-year low in September. The OBR expects real disposable incomes to fall 0.6% next year, and EDF forecasts a rise of more than £400 in the Ofgem price cap in January. The Bank had been expected to cut rates this year before the war. It has so far waited while the Fed, the ECB and the Bank of Japan raised rates, and governor Andrew Bailey says it is watching for inflation feeding into wages.

The Reserve Bank of Australia is widely expected to raise rates on Tuesday for the fifth time, taking the cash rate to its highest since 2008. CBA's Belinda Allen now expects real growth to slow to 1% by mid-2027, down from her earlier 1.8% forecast. Central Elections Committee head Noam Sohlberg, appearing with the Shin Bet chief and the attorney general, said the results would stand even if claims of foreign interference were proven.

SCOTUSblog

Colorado's universal preschool programme pays for up to 15 free hours a week at participating schools.

Colorado's universal preschool programme pays for up to 15 free hours a week at participating schools. Each school must sign an agreement to enrol children regardless of characteristics including sexual orientation and gender identity. Faith-based schools may give priority to families of their own faith but may not turn away LGBTQ families. The Archdiocese of Denver, two parishes and two parents say this shuts Catholic preschools out because of their religion. The 10th Circuit ruled for the state, holding that Carson v. Makin does not apply because Colorado welcomed religious schools, and that the rule is neutral and generally applicable under Employment Division v. Smith. The justices will decide two questions: how far Carson reaches, and what a challenger must show to prove a law is not generally applicable. On the second, the preschools rely on Tandon v. Newsom, arguing that preferences for low-income and disabled children are comparable secular exemptions. Colorado says those preferences serve, rather than undermine, its equal-access goal.

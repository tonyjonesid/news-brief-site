Al Jazeera · The Economist · The Guardian · WSJ Markets A.M. · WSJ What's News

Thursday's sell-off was driven by AI stocks.

Thursday's sell-off was driven by AI stocks. The Nasdaq fell 2.6%, its worst day since June, and the S&P 500 lost 1.2% to 7,663, its worst since late July. The Dow edged up and about two in three S&P 500 stocks actually rose. Meta lost about 7%. It beat revenue forecasts but missed on profit, raised its capital-spending forecast for the year to $135bn–$145bn, and said it would create a new class of stock to pay for AI. The WSJ says that funding plan set off a rotation out of AI stocks into almost everything else, from Treasurys to homebuilders. Nvidia fell 4.4% and was the biggest single drag on the index. Lam Research fell 11.8% despite beating forecasts, CoreWeave fell 10.1% on debt worries, and Intel and AMD each lost about 8%. Apple was the only "Magnificent 7" stock to rise. Asia led the fall: the Kospi lost 3.5% and the Hang Seng 3%. Brent jumped 4% to about $113 on fears that the Iran war will escalate, and passed $114.20 on Friday, its highest since May. Government bonds were the cushion. The 10-year Treasury yield fell from 5.13% to 4.97%, and WSJ Markets A.M. calls it the best day for Treasurys in more than a year. French yields kept rising, however, and the CAC 40 fell 1.4%. By Friday Asian markets had steadied. One analyst told Al Jazeera the swing reflected sentiment, not fundamentals: "a giant wall of worry". Moderna was hit separately (see Also today).

AI sell-off meets $114 oil: stocks slide, Treasuries rally, Bitcoin slips

Al Jazeera · The Guardian

Brent hit a five-month high after Houthi strikes in Saudi Arabia on Wednesday.

Brent hit a five-month high after Houthi strikes in Saudi Arabia on Wednesday. Two people were killed at Dammam's civilian airport and two in a drone attack on an oilfield. The IRGC also said seven vessels were hit or struck mines in and near the Strait of Hormuz. The US ordered some diplomats to leave Saudi Arabia, and Reuters reports that Aramco told staff to evacuate its Dhahran headquarters. American Airlines dropped its profit forecast, citing "historic" fuel costs. Al Jazeera asked how long the world's reserves would last if fighting resumed in full. IEA members released a record 400 million barrels in March, and the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve is down to 283 million barrels, its lowest since 1982. One analyst estimates only about 400 million usable barrels remain outside China and the Gulf, nearly half of them in Japan. India's reserves cover about eight days of imports. If Hormuz closed and the Houthis blocked Saudi tankers, Kpler estimates a shortfall of about 9.3 million barrels a day. An IG analyst said that leaves "a month or two" and makes $150 oil a real possibility, with diesel hit hardest. Separately, IEA chief Fatih Birol told Al Jazeera that Europe enters winter with record-low gas stocks, with EU storage 72% full against the usual 90%. He said Europe cannot absorb another shock and faces costlier LNG "even if peace returns". Gas prices are up 140% since February, and EU ministers are reportedly discussing rationing.

Bitcoin fell below $81,000 overnight in the risk-off move, then recovered to about $81,700–$82,000 in Asian trading on Friday, testing its 50-day moving average. Gold also bounced, from $3,986 to about $4,020. About $435m of crypto futures positions were liquidated in 24 hours, $385m of them long bets. The yen briefly hit its strongest level since mid-September after hot Japanese inflation data, then fell back toward 157 per dollar. US spot Bitcoin ETFs had net outflows on Thursday after more than $1bn of inflows earlier in the week (CoinDesk gives two figures; see cross-check notes). Traders are watching the Fed meeting on 27–28 October and oil near $100 (WTI) to $113 (Brent). Trump has invoked a Cold War-era law to boost fuel production, but prices have not eased much. In a separate piece, CoinDesk notes that bitcoin's bear-market lows have come shortly after the last three US midterms, 13 to 71 days after the vote. This cycle's low ($57,850 on 1 July) came about four months before the election. Bitcoin is about 40% above that low, down about 6% over 30 days but up 26% over 90 days. CoinDesk concludes the pattern reflects bitcoin's own cycles more than an election effect.

Courts before the midterms: student IDs, a deportation consent deal, Trump v BBC

SCOTUSblog

In a short unsigned order on Thursday, the justices paused a federal judge's ruling while New Hampshire appeals.

In a short unsigned order on Thursday, the justices paused a federal judge's ruling while New Hampshire appeals. Under the 2026 law HB 323, student IDs no longer count as valid photo ID for getting a ballot. Judge Joseph Laplante had left that part of the law in place. However, he barred the secretary of state from enforcing an April directive that discouraged election officials from accepting student IDs as evidence under a separate "safety-valve" provision. The court relied on the Purcell principle that federal courts should not change election rules just before an election. Secretary of State David Scanlan argued that the order would force him to undo months of voter guides, posters and ads. The challengers, the Coalition for Open Democracy, said the order only held him to the statute and that his website already reflected it. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson was the only dissenter. She argued that an injunction against misleading statements about election rules prevents confusion rather than causing it.

SCOTUSblog

The Justice Department and Texas filed their responses to an application by Baltimore, New Haven, Columbus and the immigrant legal-services group Centro Legal de la Raza.

The Justice Department and Texas filed their responses to an application by Baltimore, New Haven, Columbus and the immigrant legal-services group Centro Legal de la Raza. The challengers want the court to pause a consent judgment entered by Judge Reed O'Connor in Wichita Falls. On the day Texas sued in June, the DOJ joined it in asking for judgment against the 2024 "administrative closure" rule. That rule lets immigration judges suspend some deportation cases, for example for crime victims seeking relief. O'Connor struck the rule down, barred any similar rule in future, and refused to let the cities intervene for lack of standing. The challengers call the judgment the product of "open collusion" that lets any agency drop a rule it dislikes. Solicitor General D. John Sauer and Texas reply that the cities have no standing. They say any harm depends on a long chain of unlikely events and that closed cases are not automatically reopened.

Al Jazeera · The Guardian

Cloudflare saw tens of thousands of attempts to exploit the AI-found PHP flaws within 45 minutes of disclosure, then hundreds of thousands, then millions.

Cloudflare saw tens of thousands of attempts to exploit the AI-found PHP flaws within 45 minutes of disclosure, then hundreds of thousands, then millions. It also saw thousands in the 10 hours before the announcement. Amazon says the bug is "broadly exploited in the wild" and that it detected an attempt on 17 October, while the reports were still under embargo. The NSA said attackers could take over servers and steal data, and ordered immediate patching across US agencies. In the UK, 32 government services were taken offline, mostly as a precaution. Former NCSC chief Ciaran Martin called it the worst vulnerability in five years. The Netherlands, Finland, France and Belgium also took government sites down, and South Korea raised its cyber alert level. The two flaws are rated 9.2 out of 10 and affect PHP 5.4 and later. Calif, working with OpenAI, and Google-owned Wiz found them. Wiz says frontier AI turned a decades-old flaw into a working exploit.

The Guardian

Finland's Licensing and Supervision Authority (LVV) ordered Google to stop work at Muhos and Otanmäki, two sites in its €13bn Finnish AI-infrastructure project.

Finland's Licensing and Supervision Authority (LVV) ordered Google to stop work at Muhos and Otanmäki, two sites in its €13bn Finnish AI-infrastructure project. Google had cleared the land without completing environmental impact assessments. At Muhos the whole 330-hectare forest, topsoil included, is gone. Conservationists estimate a further 150,000 trees were felled at Otanmäki, and residents say muddy runoff is reaching a river. Google's Finland country manager apologised, saying the company acted on its best understanding of the law. Google says it stopped work after receiving the LVV letters on 6 October and plans to replant 130 hectares, which conservationists say is too little. More than 93,000 people have signed a petition calling for datacentre regulation. Prime minister Petteri Orpo, who had made the investment a priority, called the episode a "cold shower". With 145 datacentres planned, the issue is set to feature in April's election.

Europe and Asia: Serbia's student challenge, France's protest bans, a deadly Tibet quake

Al Jazeera · The Guardian

An estimated 150,000 people marched to Slavija Square in Belgrade on Thursday for the Student List.

An estimated 150,000 people marched to Slavija Square in Belgrade on Thursday for the Student List. It is a leaderless slate of 250 political outsiders, mostly academics and professionals, trying to end 14 years of rule by Aleksandar Vucic's Serbian Progressive party (SNS). The movement grew out of protests over the 2024 Novi Sad station canopy collapse, which killed 16 people. Vucic resigned the presidency in September to lead the SNS list for prime minister in the snap election, which was not due until 2027. He calls the movement a foreign-funded coup attempt. Civil society groups say the campaign has been marred by intimidation and misuse of state resources, and prosecutors questioned six people accused of plotting election-day violence. Analysts told Al Jazeera that turnout will decide the result. Foreign policy is the student list's biggest unknown: its candidates have floated a referendum on EU membership and signalled continuity with Russia. There is no reliable polling. Polls close at 8pm on Sunday.

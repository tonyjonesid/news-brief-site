SCOTUSblog

On 10 November the justices hear Department of Labor v.

On 10 November the justices hear Department of Labor v. Sun Valley Orchards. The 3rd Circuit held that the department's practice of having its own administrative law judges decide whether farms kept their pay promises to H-2A seasonal workers violates Article III. The government argues that the cases involve "public rights" because they arise from immigration and from privileges the executive grants. The employer says they are ordinary contract disputes that only courts may decide. Columbia's Ronald Mann, writing for SCOTUSblog, expects the justices to see the case as a matter of judicial prerogative and not to feel bound by the muddled precedent. He notes that Loper Bright and Trump v. Slaughter show an appetite for curbing the administrative state, and that none of the government's cases clearly approves an agency moving money from one private party to another. He expects Justice Gorsuch and Chief Justice Roberts to be central at the argument and says the case may deal "yet another blow" to agency adjudication.

Qatar-mediated talks centre on an Iranian offer to reopen the strait if seven conditions are met, including ending the blockade and lifting sanctions. An exemption is no guarantee of safety: a Qatari LNG carrier was hit on 30 October, and more than 120 commercial ships have been struck since the war began.

ProPublica reports that Missouri's Amendment 6 passed decisively.

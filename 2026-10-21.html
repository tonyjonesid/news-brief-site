Annual CPI inflation rose from 3.1% in August to 3.5% in September, above the 3.4% economists expected.

Annual CPI inflation rose from 3.1% in August to 3.5% in September, above the 3.4% economists expected. Motor fuel was the biggest single driver: petrol rose 8.2p a litre in the month and diesel, much of which is refined in the Middle East, rose 10.7p. Air fares also fell less than usual after the summer. Clothing prices fell slightly, and food inflation held at 1.3%. The figure lands a week before chancellor John Healey's first budget. It raises most working-age benefits from April (child benefit up 95p to £28 for the first child) and lifts business rates by more than expected, which the tax firm Ryan says could bring in over £1bn extra. Because wage growth of 3.9% is higher, the triple lock means the state pension will rise by 3.9%. The Bank of England, at 3.7%, is now widely expected to raise rates when it meets on 5 November. NIESR's Charlotte O'Leary expects inflation to reach 4%, double the target, by early 2027 as fuel costs feed into food and January's energy price cap rise arrives. Healey blamed the wars in the Middle East and Europe.

Bitcoin: Wall Street's first long-run forecast and a downside-protected ETF

CoinDesk

JPMorgan Asset Management's 2027 Long-Term Capital Market Assumptions, published Tuesday, give a numerical range for bitcoin for the first time.

JPMorgan Asset Management's 2027 Long-Term Capital Market Assumptions, published Tuesday, give a numerical range for bitcoin for the first time. The base case is 10% a year over 10 years, with a bear case of -9.7% and a bull case of 17%. That compares with 5.6–6.6% for US bonds, 7.5% and 7.7% for US large and small caps, and 5.5% for gold. The authors, portfolio manager Sheena Shah and head of digital-asset strategy Dennis Cristallo, remain cautious. They say bitcoin's volatility and unstable correlations get in the way of wide use in portfolios, that it is not a diversifier, and that it is best seen as a speculative bet and a hedge against monetary easing, trading mostly like a high-beta risk asset that follows global money supply.

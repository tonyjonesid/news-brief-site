With argument set for Wednesday, December 2, the justices on Tuesday deferred until after argument Cook County's request to dismiss the challenge to its ban on AR-15-style rifles as moot. The day before, they had granted lead plaintiff Cutberto Viramontes's unexplained request to leave the case, which will now be called Khaya v. Cook County. The county argues the remaining individual challenger, Christopher Khaya, may lack standing because he wants an IMI Galil rather than an AR-15-style rifle; the challengers reply that the question covers similar semiautomatic rifles and that Khaya would also buy an AR-15 if it were legal. The order cites the 2023 Acheson Hotels case, which the court dismissed as moot after argument, but SCOTUSblog's Amy Howe judges a merits ruling more likely here because there are several plaintiffs and a companion challenge to Connecticut's ban.

Reviewing 106 emergency decisions on federal executive action since 2017, Adam Feldman finds Chief Justice Roberts the most consistent, siding with the executive about 72% to 74% of the time under Trump's first term, Biden and Trump's second term alike, with Barrett and Kavanaugh close behind. Thomas and Alito swing sharply, from 83% under Trump I to 39% and 47% under Biden and 92% under Trump II, and agreed in all 38 coded Trump II decisions; the liberals mirror them, with Jackson backing Trump II positions in only 8% of cases against 88% for Biden.

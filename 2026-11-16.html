America's grounded skies: blame and repair after the FAA ground stop

Al Jazeera · The Guardian · WSJ The 10-Point · WSJ What's News

Friday's nearly four-hour halt to all US departures, which Al Jazeera says cancelled more than 9,000 flights and stranded about 1.5 million passengers, is now a funding fight. Transportation secretary Sean Duffy says contractor L3Harris was replacing old copper lines with fibre at a remote FAA site when a router failed; Al Jazeera reports it fired off about 1,000 login attempts every four or five seconds and overloaded the network, and WSJ traced the fault to a single router at an FAA radio site in rural Arkansas. Duffy told CNN he takes responsibility for what earlier administrations left him, said the $12.5bn Congress approved in 2025 cannot pay for new telecoms, software or towers, and has asked for at least $50bn; Trump blamed Joe Biden and demanded money "FAST". Democrats counter that only about $4bn of the $12.5bn has been committed and roughly $1bn spent, with Rosa DeLauro calling the $50bn request a "ransom without a real plan". Duffy said he would like to move away from L3Harris but it owns the network, and the FAA technicians' union says its members were not involved in the work on two contractor-owned routers. A September GAO report found obsolete kit including 1980s screens and floppy drives, paper flight strips, and parts sourced on eBay. Nearly 2,000 flights were still cancelled on Sunday, and full capacity was expected by Monday.

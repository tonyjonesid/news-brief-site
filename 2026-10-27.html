Anthropic has publicly filed for a Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT.

Anthropic has publicly filed for a Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT. Al Jazeera says it could debut before US Thanksgiving, late next month, and it may be the largest IPO ever. The prospectus reports revenue rising from $41m in 2023 to $386m in 2024 and about $4.6bn in 2025, then $16.3bn in the first half of 2026 alone. Over the same half-year it reports a net loss of about $80.7bn, which Al Jazeera attributes mainly to a rise in the fair value of convertible bonds. Anthropic had raised $141.1bn by the end of June and carries $6.2bn of debt. Its customers are mostly businesses: more than 14,000 enterprise clients, about 250 of them paying over $10m a year. The top five customers supply a quarter of revenue, and no single customer more than 10%. The risk section warns that its strongest models could meaningfully help people trying to build chemical or biological weapons. It says its safeguards are incomplete and have been circumvented before, and that in tests its models have shown self-preserving behaviour "resembling blackmail". It also says its own use of AI to speed up research could push capabilities ahead of safety work. The filing names a Chinese state-backed group that used Claude Code against about 30 targets in 2025, and the administration's designation of Anthropic as a supply-chain risk, which bars military agencies from contracting with it and which Anthropic is challenging. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead 22 underwriters. The share count and price are not yet set. Valuations of about $1.5tn are circulating, down from roughly $2tn a week ago according to Al Jazeera. The WSJ's 10-Point adds that banks demanded tougher terms before committing $42bn to a $63bn chip-leasing package for Anthropic. Broadcom chief Hock Tan closed the deal at the last minute by accepting higher interest rates and guaranteeing against an Anthropic default.

Anthropic files to go public as lenders and markets get choosier about AI

CoinDesk · The Guardian

Meta will raise $35bn through underwritten share offerings.

Meta will raise $35bn through underwritten share offerings. Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom may take up to $5bn as cornerstone investors. A further $60bn will come from an at-the-market programme selling its new non-voting class C shares, so Zuckerberg keeps control. Elon Musk said debt markets had almost entirely closed to companies without substantial revenue outside AI datacentres, and predicted "guppy-level death and consolidation" among more than 200 neocloud firms. SpaceX itself signed a $40bn chip-leasing deal with Apollo. In a 23 October note, J.P. Morgan Private Bank strategist Justin Biemann wrote that the AI label alone no longer reassures lenders. He said an unnamed debt-dependent AI infrastructure firm now has five-year credit default swaps at 1,100 basis points, which implies roughly a 50% chance of default over five years. He added that protection on cash-rich AI leaders, at 50–120bp, still looks like insurance rather than distress. Morgan Stanley data show weekly CDS trading on big AI names topped $9bn in September, up from under $2bn in July. CoinDesk notes that the Nasdaq is down 6.7% since 6 October. It also argues that AI turbulence could spill into bitcoin, because the two rallies began together after ChatGPT's launch.

Brazil after Lula's upset: a currency rout and a grudging concession

Al Jazeera · The Guardian

Lula won with 64.2m votes to 59.8m.

Lula won with 64.2m votes to 59.8m. That is at least 10m more votes than he received in the first round, and no first-round loser had won the run-off since military rule ended. At a São Paulo rally he promised "dictatorship never again". On Monday, Liberal party leader Valdemar Costa Neto said the party recognised the result but wanted "the issues raised" clarified. Flávio Bolsonaro has still not conceded. Supreme Court justice Alexandre de Moraes cancelled his passports, including his diplomatic one, and barred him from leaving Brazil. The order cites a federal police money-laundering inquiry and bans contact with a lawyer linked to betting firms suspected of laundering about £680m. Bolsonaro said the order followed his US-based brother Eduardo saying Flávio should leave if he lost, and Bolsonaro insisted he had never planned to go. Folha reports that his advisers talked him out of seeking an annulment, though he says he will file a case to disqualify Lula's ticket. An Insper analysis found that the two-hour extension to voting added about 35,000 votes, far less than Lula's 4.4m lead. Trump said the vote "looked like another rigged election" but that he "didn't take sides". Elon Musk questioned the maths. Leaders from Mexico's Sheinbaum to Chile's Kast and Colombia's de la Espriella recognised the win, and China said it respected the outcome.

Trump's Justice Department in the courts: Comey charged again, a ballot fight lost, voter-fraud bounties

The Guardian · WSJ Politics

On Monday the Justice Department charged the former FBI director with two counts of leaking national-defence information.

On Monday the Justice Department charged the former FBI director with two counts of leaking national-defence information. The charges say he conspired with an unnamed person to pass material to a news outlet in 2016, and his lawyer Patrick Fitzgerald links them to an October 2016 New York Times article that prosecutors had already investigated. The case was brought by "information", without a grand jury, apparently to beat the 10-year limitation period. Comey's lawyers say the time limit requires an indictment and that the matter was twice closed without charges. Of the earlier cases, the lying-to-Congress charge was thrown out because the prosecutor was unlawfully appointed, and the "8647" seashell case is still pending. The same day, Judge Jesse Furman ruled that Maurene Comey, a federal prosecutor in Manhattan for a decade, was illegally fired in July 2025. He rejected the claim that the president can remove prosecutors at will as contrary to nearly two centuries of Supreme Court precedent. He ordered retroactive reinstatement and back pay through 20 December.

Britain on the eve of Healey's budget: prices rising, firms struggling, banks braced

The Guardian

The British Retail Consortium says shop price inflation rose to 2.1% in October from 1.4%.

The British Retail Consortium says shop price inflation rose to 2.1% in October from 1.4%. Food inflation rose to 2.9% and non-food inflation more than doubled to 1.7%. Chief executive Helen Dickinson says "the dam is starting to break" as retailers pass on energy and ingredient costs that have climbed since the Iran war began. CPI is 3.5%, and the Bank of England is expected to raise rates from 3.75% to 4% next week. Separately, the insolvency firm BTG counts 738,144 businesses in significant distress in the third quarter, the most since its records began in 2004. Support services, construction and real estate have the most firms in distress, and hospitality had the sharpest rise, up 5%. Businesses also owe HMRC £27.3bn in tax arrears. A University of Glasgow study of low-income families found that 63% had left the heating off when cold. Its author urged Chancellor John Healey to restore the real value of child benefit in Wednesday's budget.

Bitcoin stuck near $80,000: Galaxy says buy, Calacanis says sell

CoinDesk

Galaxy's head of research, Alex Thorn, argues that the bear market ended in June below $58,000 and that bitcoin, now near $80,000 (up 38% from the low but about 35% below its record above $126,000), is in the accumulation phase that has come before past rallies.

Galaxy's head of research, Alex Thorn, argues that the bear market ended in June below $58,000 and that bitcoin, now near $80,000 (up 38% from the low but about 35% below its record above $126,000), is in the accumulation phase that has come before past rallies. His evidence is that the price has closed above its 50-week moving average for five straight weeks after 44 weeks below it. He also points to net long CME futures positions down 57% from the peak, a sign that fast money has left, and to spot ETFs adding 87,000 BTC since June. Rising US debt and interest costs, he says, add to bitcoin's appeal as a fixed-supply asset. Galaxy would rethink if bitcoin closed a week below $76,540 and did not recover within a month. On the other side, investor Jason Calacanis told followers to sell, predicting a return to $57,000 within 12 months and no new high. He cited ETF outflows of about $774m on Thursday and Friday, partly offset by $110m of inflows on Monday, and said AI now offers better uses for the money. He disclosed a stake in an AI-linked token.

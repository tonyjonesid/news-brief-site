On Wednesday evening US district judge David Carter declined to order California to segregate and hold back ballots from people the administration says are non-citizens.

On Wednesday evening US district judge David Carter declined to order California to segregate and hold back ballots from people the administration says are non-citizens. About two weeks earlier the department had handed Los Angeles and Sacramento officials a USB drive said to list 20,000 non-citizens on a roll of more than 23 million voters, gave them 48 hours, refused to explain its method, and sued on 15 October. Los Angeles officials found early signs the list was wrong. Carter wrote that the federal government had never before tried, on the eve of an election, to force a state to drop its own election procedures, that a segregated ballot is in effect a removed ballot, and that granting the request would probably breach a 1993 law barring purges within 90 days of an election. The department had tried to go around him by appealing to the ninth circuit, which ruled against it 2-1 earlier in the week. The Guardian also reports from Butte county, where clerk-recorder Keaton Denlay says a DHS agent delivered a two-page list of 47 "likely" non-citizens, full of errors and with his name misspelled, alongside a letter warning that officials who count such ballots risk becoming accomplices to a federal crime. Denlay says he will not act on it.

Just before 9am on Wednesday, near Urda, south of Madrid, a Renfe train carrying 397 people hit the back of a stalled Iryo train carrying 147.

Just before 9am on Wednesday, near Urda, south of Madrid, a Renfe train carrying 397 people hit the back of a stalled Iryo train carrying 147. Both were on the Madrid–Andalucía line. At about 8.30am a computer fault had knocked out the LZB signalling system, which automatically keeps trains apart. Control passed to Adif's centre in Madrid, which cleared the Renfe train to resume normal speed even though a stop signal was showing. Rules normally cap speeds at 40km/h in that situation. The train reached more than 200km/h before the impact. Transport minister Óscar Puente called the order "inexplicable" and said the drivers were not to blame. It is Europe's deadliest rail crash since Santiago de Compostela in 2013 and Spain's second fatal high-speed crash this year. Three days of national mourning have begun, drivers held a planned strike over safety, high-speed services to Andalucía are suspended, and the PP has called for an independent review of the whole network.

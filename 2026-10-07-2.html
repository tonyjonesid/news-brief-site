Pike, 50, is awake and speaking in a Nashville hospital a week after two doses of pentobarbital failed to kill her, the first known survivor of a completed US lethal-injection attempt. Still shackled to her bed with severe arm damage, she faces a long recovery. Her lawyers went to Davidson County Chancery Court on Wednesday for an order preserving drug lot numbers, needles and logs, arguing the IV lines were misplaced so the caustic drug pooled in her tissue, a risk they had flagged months earlier because of her small veins and a clotting disorder, which the Tennessee Supreme Court dismissed as "speculative". Governor Bill Lee ordered a third-party review and paused the state's other 2026 execution, and corrections chief Frank Strada is leaving this month. Her team wants the sentence commuted to life without parole and says a second attempt would be cruel and unusual and could breach double jeopardy. ProPublica notes this was Tennessee's third botched execution in 14 months and that ageing prisoners, questionable execution-team competence and secrecy over drug sourcing make repeats likely; prisoners facing execution in Texas and Florida this month are citing Pike's case.

Dozens of suits against OpenAI, over a teenager's suicide, an attempted overdose, a murder-suicide and two school shootings, rely on product liability, claiming ChatGPT is defectively designed.

Dozens of suits against OpenAI, over a teenager's suicide, an attempted overdose, a murder-suicide and two school shootings, rely on product liability, claiming ChatGPT is defectively designed. In a September 25 motion to dismiss a Florida case, OpenAI's Wilson Sonsini lawyers argue "information and ideas are not products", that ChatGPT is an interactive service, and that the First Amendment bars holding information providers liable for acts they inspire. Plaintiffs' counsel say an LLM has no human speaker. If courts treat chatbots as products, the test is safe design and warnings; as services, plaintiffs must prove a duty to protect users from foreseeable harm. About two dozen cases are consolidated in San Francisco Superior Court with OpenAI's response due October 30, and one plaintiffs' firm says it has 30 more in the pipeline. A judge rejected the free-speech defence in a comparable Character.AI case last year before it settled.

In a Reuters/Ipsos poll of 3,526 registered voters ending Monday, 57% said the administration has not taken AI risks seriously enough, including a third of Republicans, and 54% said the same of Congress. 84% see AI as a threat to American workers, more than illegal immigration (60%) or large corporations (73%); 62% fear it could get out of control.

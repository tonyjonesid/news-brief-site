Since July 2025, ICE has held everyone who entered without inspection, however long ago, under a provision that bars bond, a policy that helped push detention to a reported record of about 73,000 in January.

Since July 2025, ICE has held everyone who entered without inspection, however long ago, under a provision that bars bond, a policy that helped push detention to a reported record of about 73,000 in January. A SCOTUSblog analysis explains that nine circuits reject that reading of the statute and two (the 5th and 8th) accept it. The justices agreed on 1 October to hear Rhoney v Barbosa da Cunha, on the statutory question, with a decision due by the end of the term. A separate constitutional split sits behind it: the full 5th Circuit held on 23 October that such detainees have no due-process right to a bond hearing, while the 3rd and 6th Circuits say they do. The court appears to be holding the government's Putra v Lopez-Campos petition, which raises both questions, until Rhoney is decided. Until then, whether a long-term resident can ask a judge for bond depends on where they are held.

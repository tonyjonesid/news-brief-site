Election day: the Supreme Court refuses Trump on California ballots

SCOTUSblog · The Guardian

On Monday the Trump administration asked the Supreme Court to order California to keep separate the mail ballots of about 20,000 registered voters the justice department says are probably not citizens.

On Monday the Trump administration asked the Supreme Court to order California to keep separate the mail ballots of about 20,000 registered voters the justice department says are probably not citizens. Shortly before 6pm the court refused in a brief unsigned order, with no noted dissents. In a 19-page filing, Solicitor General D John Sauer argued that once the challenged ballots were mixed with lawful ones they could never be retrieved, and that California's own law allows challenged ballots to be held apart. He also asked for an immediate administrative injunction. The request went first to Justice Kagan, who referred it to the full court. Lower courts had already refused: District Judge David Carter on 28 October, who questioned the evidence and wrote that the federal government had never before tried, on the eve of an election, to coerce states into abandoning their own procedures, and then the 9th Circuit. The Guardian reports that the DoJ gave Los Angeles and Sacramento officials its list on a USB drive with 48 hours to review it and would not explain how it was compiled. A judge has separately blocked the government from threatening election officials who count the ballots. Governor Gavin Newsom said the court had "firmly slapped down" the effort, and Attorney General Rob Bonta said the fight was not over.

Magistrate Judge Zia Faruqui dismissed charges that Nikki Floris and Bradley Benavides lied to the House judiciary committee in 2023 about the FBI's handling of Hunter Biden's laptop.

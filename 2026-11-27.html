At an extraordinary Brussels summit on Thursday, leaders were far apart on a roughly $1.76trn seven-year budget and a proposed €45bn ($49bn) loan to cover Ukraine's 2027 needs on top of last year's €90bn ($99bn) loan. Germany's Friedrich Merz said Europe must not take on more debt yet has tasks it must fund, and repeated his threat to block the budget without a collective commitment to Ukraine from every member; Bulgaria, the Czech Republic, Hungary and Slovakia oppose using funds for military aid. Italy's Giorgia Meloni said the sides were "light-years away", Hungary's Peter Magyar put the chance of a deal that day at virtually zero and doubted one by Christmas, and Dutch premier Rob Jetten said the budget must be smaller. Leaders want agreement by year-end because 2027 elections in France, Italy, Poland and elsewhere could complicate talks; the package needs unanimity and includes about $110bn for Kyiv. Early on Friday Council President Antonio Costa said 24 member states backed the new loan. The Economist notes the last budget took three summits in 2020 and that poorer states want more farm and cohesion money while Germany and the Netherlands want a leaner, innovation-focused budget.

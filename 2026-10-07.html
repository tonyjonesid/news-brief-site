Christa Pike, 50, is awake and speaking in a Nashville hospital a week after Tennessee failed to execute her with two doses of pentobarbital, her public defenders said. She remains handcuffed and shackled to the bed, with severe damage to both arms, and her prognosis is unclear. Her lawyers went to Davidson County Chancery Court on Wednesday seeking an order forcing the Department of Correction to preserve drug lot numbers, needles, logs and other evidence, arguing the IV lines were misplaced and the caustic drug pooled in her tissue rather than reaching her bloodstream, something they had warned the state and courts about months earlier because of her small veins and a clotting disorder. The Tennessee Supreme Court had called those warnings "speculative". Governor Bill Lee ordered a third-party review, postponed the state's only other 2026 execution, and corrections commissioner Frank Strada is leaving this month. Pike is the first person in the US known to survive a completed lethal-injection attempt. Her lawyers want the sentence commuted to life without parole and argue a second attempt would be cruel and unusual and could breach double-jeopardy protections. ProPublica's Keri Blakinger places the failure in a pattern: Pike's was Tennessee's third botched execution in 14 months, an ageing death-row population makes vein access harder, execution teams have faced competence questions in several states, and secrecy laws hide the age and source of drugs. Prisoners facing execution this week in Texas and next week in Florida are citing the Pike case to demand proof the drugs are sound.

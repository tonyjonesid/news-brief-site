SCOTUSblog

In Nielsen v.

In Nielsen v. Watanabe, Kekai Watanabe says a paramedic at a federal detention centre in Hawaii gave him only over-the-counter painkillers for a fractured coccyx and kept him in solitary for more than two months without specialist care. He sued under Bivens and the 1980 Carlson v. Green decision, and the 9th Circuit let the claim go ahead. At argument a majority seemed likely to dismiss it. The paramedic's lawyer urged the Court to overrule Bivens and Carlson outright, and the solicitor general's office asked it to declare Carlson obsolete or overrule it. Chief Justice Roberts and Justice Kavanaugh were wary of going that far: Kavanaugh noted that Congress has left both rulings alone for decades and floated applying them without extending them, and Roberts pointed out that the government itself had not asked for Bivens to be overruled. Justices Gorsuch, Alito and Barrett asked what other remedies would be left, such as the Federal Tort Claims Act or injunctions. Kagan said the facts looked like ordinary malpractice rather than Carlson's. Sotomayor and Jackson seemed most sympathetic to the inmate. A decision is expected by early July.

Hoffmann v.

Hoffmann v. WBI Energy Transmission asks whether a gas company that uses federal eminent-domain power under the 1947 Natural Gas Act must meet state-law compensation rules, here North Dakota's possible award of landowners' legal fees, which would not apply if the US took the land itself. Ronald Mann writes that the bench was sceptical across the board. Justice Thomas questioned whether delegated federal power carries state-law terms with it, and Chief Justice Roberts asked why it was not "an easy case". Justice Sotomayor cited an 1893 precedent that the power to take land and the duty to compensate go together. Justices Jackson, Kavanaugh and Barrett noted that Congress expressly adopted state law in a 1946 condemnation statute but said nothing in the 1947 Act, which suggests the federal baseline applies. Mann expects a quick and probably unanimous ruling for the pipeline company.

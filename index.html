Huang owns about 3.6% of the company, which also faces an FBI inquiry, congressional probes and a shareholder class action, and reports quarterly earnings on Tuesday.

Big Tech's fragility: a global Microsoft outage and a lawsuit to freeze frontier AI

Al Jazeera · The Guardian

Outlook, Teams, Copilot, SharePoint, OneDrive and the Office apps failed for users in 74 countries from Thursday evening GMT, and by Friday morning Microsoft reported only "early signs of stabilisation" with capacity still below normal.

Outlook, Teams, Copilot, SharePoint, OneDrive and the Office apps failed for users in 74 countries from Thursday evening GMT, and by Friday morning Microsoft reported only "early signs of stabilisation" with capacity still below normal. The company says some servers became unexpectedly unavailable after failing automated health checks, pushing load onto the rest, which then failed in turn; it says there is no sign of a cyber-attack, was rolling back a network change, has extended its email timeout window to 48 hours and promises a post-incident report. Australia's home affairs minister said Microsoft likened it to a botched update, as with CrowdStrike. The disruption reached England's NHS (remote therapy, pharmacy-to-GP messages), UK police online crime reporting and schools, government systems in Singapore and Japan, the Philippine Supreme Court, airlines IndiGo and Akasa Air, and Microsoft connectors used by OpenAI and Anthropic. Analysts told Al Jazeera the episode adds to Europe's push for digital sovereignty, though they expect little short-term revenue effect because customers are locked into contracts; it follows a January Microsoft 365 outage of almost 10 hours and an Azure outage last month.

The Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen, Heat Initiative and the Alliance for Secure AI filed a civil complaint in San Francisco Superior Court on Thursday seeking an emergency injunction that would pause training and deployment of next-generation models until the companies can show the systems remain under human control. They rely on California's unfair competition law, arguing the firms endanger public health and interfere with the use of property such as computer systems, and cite AI-assisted cyberattacks and the companies' own warnings about catastrophic risk. The Irreplaceable's founder said a real freeze would need government inspections, verification and monitoring of large computing facilities; the group favours independent federal evaluators, strict liability and a dedicated regulator, with states free to go further, and argues a treaty with China would keep competitiveness balanced. The plaintiffs note Congress has passed no AI law and hope the incoming Democratic majority will act, though they say courts must not wait. Separately, the Economist reports United Airlines will bar personal AI agents from booking or checking in, citing data-privacy risk.

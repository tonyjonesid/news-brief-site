Katie Martin's Unhedged note says the People's Bank of China released an unusually combative paper on its renminbi policy last week, just before trade talks with the EU. The offshore renminbi has gained more than 9 per cent against the dollar since the start of 2025, while it is roughly flat against the euro over that span. The EU runs a trade deficit with China of about €1bn a day and wants more currency adjustment. The paper rejects the idea that a weak exchange rate explains China's export strength, argues there is no agreed method for judging an equilibrium rate, says the BIS effective rate has risen sharply since 2005, and says no central bank can steer a market trading close to $10tn a day. A Bank of Singapore analyst called it the first such explicit statement and a vigorous defence of its exchange-rate management, while former US Treasury official Mark Sobel said it was "left wanting" and that the renminbi remains heavily managed. The newsletter also flags US banks' trading revenues looking set to cool and hyperscaler bond supply dropping as investor demands rise.

The Due Diligence newsletter reports that an independent panel found Manchester City guilty of breaching Premier League financial rules, saying the club overstated its position by more than £900mn over nine years via "sham" commercial deals. Sanctions could range from fines to points deductions or expulsion, and the club has rejected the ruling and appealed. The piece frames this as a strain on UK-UAE ties at a time when Prime Minister Andy Burnham wants Gulf investment to keep flowing, noting that the club's owners have invested heavily in Manchester, and quotes a former western official comparing the relationship to snakes and ladders. It also covers CVC's initially undisclosed €250mn minority stake in the Israeli camouflage maker Fibrotex, and several UK high-street brands, including Third Space, being put up for sale by private equity owners.
